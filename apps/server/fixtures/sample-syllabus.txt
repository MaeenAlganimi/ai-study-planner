Course description
CS 301 introduces the data structures and algorithm-analysis habits used in later systems and software courses. Students implement the structures themselves, argue about running time in writing, and finish with an individual graph visualizer.

Weekly topics
Week 1: Asymptotic analysis. Big-O, big-Theta, and how to bound the running time of loops and recursive functions.
Week 2: Arrays and amortized analysis. Dynamic arrays, doubling strategies, and aggregate analysis.
Week 3: Linked lists and stacks. Singly and doubly linked lists, and stack-based evaluation.
Week 4: Queues and recursion. Circular queues, tail recursion, and the call stack.
Week 5: Trees and binary search trees. Traversals, insertion, and deletion.
Week 6: AVL trees and heaps. Rotations, the heap property, and priority queues.
Week 7: Hash tables. Chaining, open addressing, and choosing a hash function.
Week 8: Graphs and traversal. Representations, breadth-first search, and depth-first search.
Week 9: Shortest paths. Dijkstra's algorithm and when it does not apply.
Week 10: Sorting. Mergesort, quicksort, and the comparison lower bound.
Week 11: Dynamic programming. Overlapping subproblems, memoization, and tabulation.
Week 12: Review and project studio. Practice exams and graph visualizer critiques.
